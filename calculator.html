      Tide is NOAA’s prediction for Port Angeles (9444090). Current is NOAA’s tidal current prediction for Angeles Point, 2 mi NNE (PUG1639). It is used for tide timing: each hour’s odds are scaled by how far it is from the nearest predicted flood start (current turning east). Since 2020 the odds of 1.0 m+ ran about 1.2–1.4× higher from the flood start to 2 h after it, and about 0.85–0.9× from 3–6 h before it. Flat-to-3 ft jumps at Angeles Point happened within an hour of a flood start more than twice as often as chance. The predicted size isn’t adjusted for the tide. Wind is the National Weather Service hourly forecast for Port Angeles (about 6 days ahead); for today it is the wind measured at Port Angeles airport (hourly), and for past dates the NOAA Port Angeles harbor station, which is sheltered and tends to read light. Light is first to last light at Port Angeles. The odds compare your swell with every Neah Bay hour since 2020 from 230–319° and 1.5 m+, and how often Angeles Point then read 1.0 m+ at 10 s+, weighting the closest height (±0.5 m), period (±2 s) and direction (±7°). The predicted size is the average height Angeles Point reached in those similar swells, in the hours around the swell’s arrival. For today and future dates the swell shown is treated as running all day, and the odds assume it holds. For a past date the swell is filled from the Neah Bay buoy (NDBC 46087): each hour uses the Neah Bay reading from 2–3 hours earlier, matched to the swell’s travel time, and the Angeles Point buoy (46267) row shows what was actually measured. Buoy history comes from this site’s hourly archive of NOAA readings.
